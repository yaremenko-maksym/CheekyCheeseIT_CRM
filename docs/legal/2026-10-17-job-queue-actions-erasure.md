# Erasure vs an append-only staff audit log (`job_queue_actions`)

**Mode:** consult (A) · **Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Author:** Legal agent (AI preliminary check)
**Overall confidence:** MED. Each conclusion carries its own tag below.
**Question from:** Master, on behalf of the owner. Pseudonymisation is approved as the engineering direction, on condition of this answer.

> This is an AI preliminary check, not binding legal advice. Escalation status is in §8.

## 1. TL;DR

- **Pseudonymising the `users` row is not erasure.** For the company it produces pseudonymised data, which is still personal data (GDPR Recital 26; UA Law 2297-VI art. 2, which requires removing _indirect_ identifiers too). The direction still holds, but as a **data-minimisation measure for a lawful retention period**, not as the answer to an erasure request. **[HIGH on the principle, MED on how it applies to this repo]**
- **Option (a) is not enough on its own, and option (b) is not needed.** Use a **hybrid**: (a) for `actor_id` / `subject_user_id` while the log is retained, **plus** a narrow logged redaction path for `reason`, **plus** an end-of-retention path (delete the rows, or re-point them to one shared "former staff" sentinel). A log that is append-only _forever_ breaks the storage-limitation rule under both laws, whichever option is chosen. **[MED]**

## 4. Q1: Is `actor_id` personal data?

**Yes, under both laws. [HIGH]**

- **GDPR.** [Art. 4(1)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/) covers a person identifiable "by reference to an identifier", and an internal UUID that the company itself resolves through a join is exactly that. The row also says what that person did and when, which is information _relating to_ them (a record of work behaviour). Recital 26 assesses identifiability using "all the means reasonably likely to be used" ([Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/)).
- **UA.** [Law 2297-VI art. 2](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2297-17) defines personal data as information about a person "яка ідентифікована або може бути конкретно ідентифікована". The company can do this through the `users` join.
- The UUID being random does not matter. What matters is that **the company holds the key**.

## 5. Q2: Does option (a) satisfy an erasure request?

### 5.1 GDPR: option (a) is pseudonymisation, not erasure, from the company's point of view

**Conclusion: option (a) as described is not erasure. The pseudonymised log is still personal data for the company, so keeping it needs a lawful basis (§6). [HIGH on the principle, MED on applying it]**

1. **The law.** Recital 26 says pseudonymised data that can be attributed to a person using additional information is still information about an identifiable person. Only anonymous information falls outside the GDPR ([Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/); definition in [Art. 4(5)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/)). EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 on pseudonymisation (adopted 16 Jan 2025 for consultation) say the same: pseudonymised data is still personal data, and pseudonymisation is a safeguard, not anonymisation. (WebSearch: https://www.hunton.com/insights/publications/edpb-advises-on-pseudonymisation-for-gdpr-compliance, collected 2026-10-17. Commentary only. I could not confirm a final adopted version, so verify against edpb.europa.eu.)
2. **Whose point of view counts.** The CJEU in _EDPS v SRB_, **C-413/23 P** (judgment of 4 Sept 2025), held that pseudonymised data may stop being personal data **for a recipient** who cannot re-identify. It stays personal data **for the controller that holds the additional information** (Commentary: https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/cjeu-clarifies-concept-of-personal-data-for-a-transfer-of-pseudonymised-data-to-third-parties and https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/cjeu-clarifies-concepts-personal-and-pseudonymised-data-implications-data-sharing, collected 2026-10-17. Law-firm summaries only; read the judgment paragraphs before relying on them). Here the company is the original controller, so the recipient-side relief does not help.
3. **Does option (a) actually destroy the "additional information"?** Strictly, option (a) overwrites the key rather than keeping it separately, so the question becomes whether the remaining data is _anonymous_ under the "means reasonably likely" test. It is not, because the repository shows at least four ways back to the person:
   - **R3:** `user_audit_log.changes` keeps past `displayName` values in plain text, under the same UUID. This alone defeats option (a).
   - **R2:** the same UUID is a foreign key from transactions, contracts/documents and team membership. Several of these **must** keep identity for accounting and tax reasons (§6.3), so the company can rejoin the UUID to a named person at any time.
   - **R4:** `user_emails` and `google_id` are outside the field list in the brief ("name, email, phone, photo reference and similar").
   - **Context:** in a small studio, team membership, timestamps and reassignment patterns single the person out to colleagues. The WP29 anonymisation tests (singling out, linkability, inference) would fail. WP29 states plainly that "pseudonymisation is not a method of anonymisation" ([WP29 Opinion 05/2014 on Anonymisation Techniques, WP216](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2014/wp216_en.pdf)). **[MED: depends on team size, F7]**
4. **Why this does not sink the direction.** [Art. 17](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/) is not a duty to delete everything on request. It applies only where one of the grounds in 17(1) is met: the data is no longer necessary (a), consent is withdrawn (b), a successful objection under Art. 21 (c), the processing is unlawful (d), or a legal obligation requires erasure (e). It also gives way to the exceptions in 17(3), in particular legal obligation (b) and legal claims (e). If the log is still needed for a documented purpose, the controller **may refuse erasure of the log for that period**, and option (a) is then a good minimisation step on top. Erasure becomes due when the purpose expires. At that point you need deletion or real anonymisation, and option (a) provides neither.
5. **The reply to the data subject has to be honest.** Under [Art. 12(4)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-12-gdpr/), if erasure is partly refused, the person must be told why and of their right to complain. Describing option (a) to them as "your data has been erased" would be inaccurate.

### 5.2 Ukraine: the UA rule on leaving is stricter

**Conclusion: option (a) is not «знеособлення» under UA law, and the UA law presumes deletion when the relationship ends. [MED]**

- Law 2297-VI art. 2 defines «знеособлення персональних даних» as removing information "які дають змогу **прямо чи опосередковано** ідентифікувати особу" (directly **or indirectly**). Because of R2, R3 and the context above, indirect identification survives option (a). It is therefore not знеособлення ([Law 2297-VI](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2297-17/print), current version 14.06.2025, collected 2026-10-17).
- **Art. 15(2)(2):** personal data must be deleted or destroyed when the legal relationship between the data subject and the controller ends, «якщо інше не передбачено законом» (unless the law provides otherwise). For a departing FOP contractor, this is a **default duty to delete**, triggered by leaving and **not** by any request. The GDPR has no equivalent automatic trigger.
- **Art. 6(8):** data may be kept "у формі, що допускає ідентифікацію" (in an identifiable form) no longer than is needed for the lawful purposes.
- **Art. 8(2)(5)–(6):** the person may object to processing and may make a reasoned demand to change or destroy their data.
- **Open point. [LOW]** It is not settled whether «інше не передбачено законом» can be met by the controller's _legitimate interest_ (art. 11(1)(6)) or needs a specific statutory retention rule. I found no authoritative commentary or court practice. This decides whether the CRM's current **"archive, never delete"** policy for `users` is defensible for Ukrainian staff at all. That question is **wider than this log**, and a human lawyer should look at it (§8).

### 6.3 Is there a Ukrainian retention duty that overrides erasure for this log?

**Probably not. [LOW to MED]**

- **Tax.** The Tax Code (ПКУ) п. 44.3 requires keeping documents used for tax accounting for at least 1095 days. Secondary sources report a later increase to 1825 days, which I could not confirm against the current text. (WebSearch: https://kpmg.com/ua/uk/home/media/press-releases/2023/09/novi-stroky-zberihannya-dokumentiv.html and https://profpressa.com/news/zmineno-termini-zberigannya-pervinnikh-dokumentiv-pidpisano-zakon/print, collected 2026-10-17. Verify on [zakon.rada.gov.ua ПКУ](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2755-17).) A record of who opened or took a vacancy **is not a primary accounting document**. The invoice or act for a resulting placement is, but that lives in the finance and documents tables, not here.
- **Archive and personnel.** Minjust Order No. 578/5 of 12.04.2012 («Перелік типових документів…», registered as 571/20884) sets retention periods for standard records of legal entities. One source says it is only advisory for FOPs. (WebSearch: https://i.factor.ua/ukr/info/biudzhetnyku/stroky-zberihannia-dokumentiv/, collected 2026-10-17.) I did not find a category that clearly covers an internal CRM action log, and I did not verify individual items.
- **Labour law.** Staff are FOP contractors under civil-law contracts (R6), so employment record-keeping rules (КЗпП) generally do not apply. This is **[MED]** and depends on there being no recharacterisation risk (see `.claude/knowledge/legal/domain-lessons.md`, the SENIOR template risks).
- **Net effect.** Keeping the log after the contract ends rests on **legitimate interest / legal claims, not on a statutory duty**. In Ukraine this is exactly where the art. 15(2)(2) «якщо інше не передбачено законом» question in §5.2 arises.

## 7. Q4: The free-text `reason` field

**Yes, a named third party has rights that option (a) cannot reach. The narrow fix is to redact `reason` only. [HIGH on the right existing, MED on the remedy]**

- A third party named in `reason` is a data subject in their own right: UA art. 8(2)(5)–(6), and GDPR Art. 17 and 21 where the GDPR applies (§3). Overwriting the _actor's_ `users` row does nothing to text written about someone else.
- **The departing staff member is affected too.** Colleagues may write "reassigned because Olena is on sick leave". Option (a) does not remove the actor's own name from other people's `reason` text. "On sick leave" is health data ([GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/); UA art. 7 sets a special regime for health data), which raises the stakes.
- **Remedy.** A redaction path for `reason` **only**. Replace the text with a fixed token. Do not set it to NULL, so the NOT NULL constraint and the meaning of the row both survive. Make this the only UPDATE the trigger permits, available only through one privileged function, and have each redaction **append** a `REASON_REDACTED` event recording who redacted, when, and under which request ID. The log then still shows _that_ something changed and by whom. That is a defensible form of integrity: provably complete, rather than unchangeable.
- **Prevention is cheaper than redaction** (data minimisation, [Art. 5(1)(c)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/)). Make the reason a **required code from a fixed list**, with free text optional, short, and shown with a UI hint such as "do not name people". Apply the same contact-stripping the plan already calls "sanitized reason" (R1), for example removing e-mails and phone numbers. Whether free text is needed at all is a product decision. If it is not, drop it.

## 8. Q5: Recommendation

**A hybrid. Neither (a) alone nor (b). [MED]**

1. **Keep option (a) for `actor_id` / `subject_user_id` during the retention period**, framed and named as **pseudonymisation**, not erasure. It is a good safeguard. The pseudonymisation routine must cover **everything that identifies the person**, not just the core `users` fields: the `users` profile and requisite fields, all `user_emails` rows, `google_id`, avatar and identity documents (S3 objects included), and **the plain-text `displayName` history in `user_audit_log.changes` (R3)**. Without R3 the measure does not achieve its goal even as pseudonymisation.
2. **Fix R3 in any case**, separately from this feature. Add `displayName` to `SENSITIVE_FIELDS` going forward, and decide what to do with existing rows. This is a Coder task with security-reviewer, because it touches `apps/api/src/users/**`.
3. **Narrow logged redaction path for `reason` only** (§7). Leave `actor_id` / `subject_user_id` NOT NULL and untouchable during retention. Option (b)'s general mutability is not needed.
4. **An end-of-retention path through the trigger.** When an event kind's retention expires (§6.2), either DELETE the rows, or re-point `actor_id` / `subject_user_id` to one shared "former staff" sentinel user. The second keeps aggregate counts but destroys linkability between rows and back to the person. Either way the trigger needs a second, narrow, privileged exception, executed only by the retention job. **This is required whichever option is chosen**, because append-only forever is not compliant (§6.2).
5. **Consider `ON DELETE SET NULL` instead of `RESTRICT`** for consistency with the existing audit tables (R5). Under an archive-only policy it makes no practical difference today. It removes a trap if hard deletion of users is ever introduced to meet UA art. 15(2)(2).
6. **Paperwork** (cheap, and it makes the position defensible): a written legitimate-interest balancing test for the log; a retention schedule (§6.2); a staff privacy notice that mentions the log (UA art. 12 duty to inform; GDPR [Art. 13](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/) if it applies); and a template reply for partial refusal of erasure (§5.1 point 5).

**Does the owner's condition hold?** Yes, as an **engineering direction**. It does not hold as a claim that "pseudonymisation equals erasure". The direction needs items 1–4 above to be compliant.

## 10. Escalation

- **No hard-refuse zone has been triggered** (`.claude/knowledge/legal/cross-cutting/escalation-zones.md`): no court, no dispute with a state body, no criminal exposure. This assumes F6 is "no request is pending".
- **Soft zone: a DPIA for a new feature.** I recommend a practising privacy lawyer validate two points **before** this design is relied on for an actual erasure request:
  1. the UA art. 15(2)(2) question, i.e. whether legitimate interest counts as «інше передбачено законом». It decides whether "archive, never delete" is lawful for Ukrainian staff, which **goes beyond this log**;
  2. GDPR territorial scope, **if F1 shows an EU establishment**.
- **Upgrades to hard-refuse (lawyer first) if:** a former staff member makes an erasure demand while in a pay or attribution dispute (zone 6, active dispute), or the Ombudsman issues an order (art. 15(2)(3)).
- **Law in flux.** Draft Law No. 8153 (a GDPR-aligned replacement for Law 2297-VI) passed first reading on 20 Nov 2024 and, per the latest sources found, is not yet enacted (WebSearch: https://voxukraine.org/en/mandatory-informing-control-and-high-penalties-how-the-new-draft-law-changes-the-rules-of-personal-data-protection and https://www.linklaters.com/en/insights/data-protected/data-protected---ukraine, collected 2026-10-17). If it is enacted, recheck §5.2: the UA rules would move towards the GDPR model (purpose-based retention and Art. 17-style exceptions).
