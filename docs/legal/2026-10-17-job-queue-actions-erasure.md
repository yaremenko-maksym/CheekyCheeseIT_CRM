## 1. TL;DR

- **Pseudonymising the `users` row is not erasure.** For the company it produces pseudonymised data, which is still personal data (GDPR Recital 26; UA Law 2297-VI art. 2, which requires removing _indirect_ identifiers too). The direction holds, but as a **data-minimisation measure for a lawful retention period**, not as the answer to an erasure request. **[HIGH]**
- **Option (a) is not enough on its own, and option (b) is not needed.** Use a **hybrid**: (a) for `actor_id` / `subject_user_id` while the log is retained, **plus** prevention at input for `reason` with a narrow logged redaction path as a backstop, **plus** an end-of-retention path that **deletes** expired rows. **[MED]**
- **With two-member teams, deletion is the only end-of-retention option that works.** Re-pointing `actor_id` to a shared sentinel leaves `team_id` plus `team_members` to name the person by elimination. **[HIGH]**
- **Retention: two years, uniform — decided by the owner** (§6.2). For the low-value telemetry kinds I still recommend 90 days and have recorded why; the decision stands as the decision. **Consequence:** at two years a departing contractor leaves live rows behind, so the deletion path and the open UA art. 15(2)(2) question are load-bearing, not theoretical.
- **The GDPR applies in full**, because the controller is EU-established. That does not change the table design, but it switches on obligations around it, including a Chapter V transfer tool for data reaching Ukraine. **[HIGH on applicability, MED on the transfer point]** (§3.1, §3.2).

If anything has already been signed on production, that is a lawyer question about who is bound, not a code fix (§10). Production is not visible from here.

## 4. Q1: Is `actor_id` personal data?

**Yes, under both laws. [HIGH]**

- **GDPR.** [Art. 4(1)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/) covers a person identifiable "by reference to an identifier", and an internal UUID that the company itself resolves through a join is exactly that. The row also says what that person did and when, which is information _relating to_ them (a record of work behaviour). Recital 26 assesses identifiability using "all the means reasonably likely to be used" ([Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/)).
- **UA.** [Law 2297-VI art. 2](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2297-17) defines personal data as information about a person "яка ідентифікована або може бути конкретно ідентифікована". The company can do this through the `users` join.
- The UUID being random does not matter. What matters is that **the company holds the key**.

## 5. Q2: Does option (a) satisfy an erasure request?

### 5.1 GDPR: option (a) is pseudonymisation, not erasure

**Conclusion: option (a) as described is not erasure. The pseudonymised log is still personal data for the company, so keeping it needs a lawful basis (§6). [HIGH]**

1. **The law.** Recital 26 says pseudonymised data that can be attributed to a person using additional information is still information about an identifiable person. Only anonymous information falls outside the GDPR ([Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/); definition in [Art. 4(5)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/)). EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 on pseudonymisation (adopted 16 Jan 2025 for consultation) say the same: pseudonymised data is still personal data, and pseudonymisation is a safeguard, not anonymisation. (WebSearch: https://www.hunton.com/insights/publications/edpb-advises-on-pseudonymisation-for-gdpr-compliance, collected 2026-10-17. Commentary only; a final adopted version could not be confirmed, so verify against edpb.europa.eu.)
2. **Whose point of view counts.** The CJEU in _EDPS v SRB_, **C-413/23 P** (judgment of 4 Sept 2025), held that pseudonymised data may stop being personal data **for a recipient** who cannot re-identify. It stays personal data **for the controller that holds the additional information** (Commentary: https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/cjeu-clarifies-concept-of-personal-data-for-a-transfer-of-pseudonymised-data-to-third-parties and https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/cjeu-clarifies-concepts-personal-and-pseudonymised-data-implications-data-sharing, collected 2026-10-17. Law-firm summaries only; read the judgment paragraphs before relying on them). Here the company is the original controller, so the recipient-side relief does not help.
3. **Does option (a) actually destroy the "additional information"? No — and with two-member teams it is not close. [HIGH]** Option (a) overwrites the key rather than keeping it separately, so the question is whether what remains is _anonymous_ under the "means reasonably likely" test. It is not. In descending order of how hard the route is to close:
   - **Structural, via team scope.** Every scoped row carries `team_id`, and the action log also carries `subject_team_id`. A senior's team is normally **two people, the senior and one HR** (F7). So a TEAM-scoped action identifies its actor **by elimination**: if it is not the senior, it is the HR. The membership needed for that inference is in `team_members`, which is not touched by option (a) and which **keeps departed members** through the `left_at` soft delete (R10). This is not a contextual guess about colleagues' memories; it is a two-table join. **With k = 2, scope plus membership is an identifier.**
   - **R3:** `user_audit_log.changes` keeps past `displayName` values in plain text, under the same UUID. This alone defeats option (a).
   - **R2:** the same UUID is a foreign key from transactions, contracts/documents and team membership. Several of these **must** keep identity for accounting and tax reasons (§6.3), so the company can rejoin the UUID to a named person at any time.
   - **R4:** `user_emails` and `google_id` are outside the field list in the proposal ("name, email, phone, photo reference and similar").

   The WP29 anonymisation tests all fail: singling out (the team-scope join), linkability (the shared UUID across tables) and inference. WP29 states plainly that "pseudonymisation is not a method of anonymisation" ([WP29 Opinion 05/2014 on Anonymisation Techniques, WP216](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2014/wp216_en.pdf)).

   **At what team size does this bite?** The argument is strongest at **k = 2**, where elimination is certain, and it stays decisive at **k ≤ 5**, where a single additional cue (a timestamp against a holiday, a role only one member holds) usually closes the gap. From roughly **k ≥ 10** the scope stops being an identifier on its own, and the other routes above become the binding ones — which still defeat option (a), only less sharply. Since teams here are two people, the conclusion is firm rather than contingent. **[MED on the thresholds — judgment by analogy to k-anonymity, not a statutory test; the k = 2 conclusion itself is HIGH]**

4. **Why this does not sink the direction.** [Art. 17](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/) is not a duty to delete everything on request. It applies only where one of the grounds in 17(1) is met: the data is no longer necessary (a), consent is withdrawn (b), a successful objection under Art. 21 (c), the processing is unlawful (d), or a legal obligation requires erasure (e). It also gives way to the exceptions in 17(3). While the log is still within its retention period for a documented purpose, the controller **may refuse erasure of it**, and option (a) is a good minimisation step on top. **Erasure becomes due when that period expires — which, at the decided two years (§6.2), will routinely fall after the person has left.** At that point deletion or real anonymisation is required, and option (a) provides neither.
5. **The reply to the data subject has to be honest.** Under [Art. 12(4)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-12-gdpr/), if erasure is partly refused, the person must be told why and of their right to complain. Describing option (a) to them as "your data has been erased" would be inaccurate.

### 5.2 Ukraine: the rule on leaving is stricter

**Conclusion: option (a) is not «знеособлення» under UA law, and the UA law presumes deletion when the relationship ends. [MED]**

- Law 2297-VI art. 2 defines «знеособлення персональних даних» as removing information "які дають змогу **прямо чи опосередковано** ідентифікувати особу" (directly **or indirectly**). Because of the four routes in §5.1 point 3, indirect identification survives option (a). It is therefore not знеособлення ([Law 2297-VI](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2297-17/print), current version 14.06.2025, collected 2026-10-17).
- **Art. 15(2)(2):** personal data must be deleted or destroyed when the legal relationship between the data subject and the controller ends, «якщо інше не передбачено законом» (unless the law provides otherwise). For a departing FOP contractor this is a **default duty to delete**, triggered by leaving and **not** by any request. The GDPR has no equivalent automatic trigger.
- **Art. 6(8):** data may be kept "у формі, що допускає ідентифікацію" (in an identifiable form) no longer than is needed for the lawful purposes.
- **Art. 8(2)(5)–(6):** the person may object to processing and may make a reasoned demand to change or destroy their data.
- **Open point. [LOW]** It is not settled whether «інше не передбачено законом» can be met by the controller's _legitimate interest_ (art. 11(1)(6)) or needs a specific statutory retention rule. No authoritative commentary or court practice was found. This decides whether the CRM's **"archive, never delete"** policy for `users` is defensible for Ukrainian staff at all. The question is **wider than this log**, and a human lawyer should look at it (§10). **At a two-year retention it arises in the ordinary case, not the exceptional one** (§6.2).
- **A two-year period, once stated, also helps.** Art. 15(2)(1) triggers deletion on expiry of a storage period "визначеного згодою … або законом". The draft ToS §6.5, where retention periods belong, is **blank** (R7). Writing the decided period there converts a vague position into a stated one, which helps under both laws (§8 item 6).

An earlier draft of this memo floated "contract term + 3 years" for attribution events, on a legal-claims basis. **That is superseded in both directions:** F5 removed the basis, and the decided two years is shorter. **A uniform figure has a real virtue beyond simplicity of code.** One period is one rule: statable in a privacy notice in a sentence, implementable as one predicate, auditable by one query. Graduated schedules drift — a kind gets added, nobody re-reads the table, rows outlive the schedule silently. Against that failure mode, "two years for everything, deleted by a job" is a more reliable form of compliance than a well-argued four-tier table nobody maintains. **[MED]**

**Where the recommendation still differs from the decision. [MED]**

- **Two years for OPENED / DEAD_LINK / SPAM is hard to defend on the stated purpose.** "Who clicked a vacancy link" has no evidential value after a few weeks, the only basis is legitimate interest, and under [Art. 5(1)(c)/(e)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/) the question is always "necessary for what?". Two years is roughly eight times the 90 days the adjacent postings already use, which invites exactly the question a regulator asks. **The recommendation remains 90 days for these three kinds**, which would also keep the log smaller.
- **For the other kinds two years is defensible**, if generous. An abuse-investigation and conflict-resolution margin of two years is arguable for a studio that does not review this log weekly.
- **If a single number is preferred over a split, the safer single number is shorter.** "Twelve months for everything" would be uniform _and_ comfortably defensible across all kinds. Two years uniform is the more exposed of the two uniform options.
- **Recorded as the decision regardless.** This is the owner's call, it is written down, and it is implementable. A documented decision with a stated purpose is a far better position than an undocumented one, whatever the figure.

**Three consequences of a two-year period, stated plainly:**

- **Retention now outlasts the relationship in the ordinary case.** A departing contractor will normally leave behind up to two years of live rows naming them. So the deletion path (§8 item 4), the pseudonymisation routine (§8 item 1) and the **unresolved UA art. 15(2)(2) question (§5.2)** are load-bearing: they will be exercised for most leavers, not occasionally. A shorter period would have let most rows expire before departure and largely dissolved the question; two years does not. **[MED]**
- **Objections get stronger with age.** Legitimate interest is the only basis (§6.1), and its weight decays as the operational purpose recedes. An Art. 21 objection to rows from twenty months ago is materially harder to resist than one to rows from last month.
- **Storage limitation still binds.** A trigger that refuses DELETE forever, with no exception, does not meet GDPR [Art. 5(1)(e)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/) or Law 2297-VI art. 6(8). **[HIGH]**

### 6.3 Is there a Ukrainian retention duty that overrides erasure for this log?

**Probably not. [LOW to MED]**

- **Tax.** The Tax Code (ПКУ) п. 44.3 requires keeping documents used for tax accounting for at least 1095 days. Secondary sources report a later increase to 1825 days, which could not be confirmed against the current text. (WebSearch: https://kpmg.com/ua/uk/home/media/press-releases/2023/09/novi-stroky-zberihannya-dokumentiv.html and https://profpressa.com/news/zmineno-termini-zberigannya-pervinnikh-dokumentiv-pidpisano-zakon/print, collected 2026-10-17. Verify on [zakon.rada.gov.ua ПКУ](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2755-17).) A record of who opened or took a vacancy **is not a primary accounting document**, and it is further from one given that it feeds no payment (F5).
- **Archive and personnel.** Minjust Order No. 578/5 of 12.04.2012 («Перелік типових документів…», registered as 571/20884) sets retention periods for standard records of legal entities. One source says it is only advisory for FOPs. (WebSearch: https://i.factor.ua/ukr/info/biudzhetnyku/stroky-zberihannia-dokumentiv/, collected 2026-10-17.) No category clearly covering an internal CRM action log was found, and individual items were not verified.
- **Labour law.** Staff are FOP contractors under civil-law contracts (R6), so employment record-keeping rules (КЗпП) generally do not apply. **[MED]**, subject to the recharacterisation risk in `.claude/knowledge/legal/domain-lessons.md`.
- **Net effect.** Keeping the log after the contract ends rests on legitimate interest alone — no statutory duty backs it. That is precisely where the art. 15(2)(2) question in §5.2 bites, and **at a two-year period it bites for most departures rather than a few**. The absence of a retention duty cuts both ways: nothing compels keeping these rows, which makes a two-year legitimate-interest claim the only thing standing between the log and a deletion demand.

## 7. Q4: The free-text `reason` field

**A named third party has rights that option (a) cannot reach [HIGH]. Because the field is pre-UI, prevention at input is the primary remedy and redaction the backstop. [HIGH on the remedy]**

- **Scope.** There are **four** planned reason columns, not one (R11): `job_queue_actions.reason`, `job_queue_dismissals.reason`, `job_queue_assignments.release_reason`, `job_team_triage_subscriptions.reason`. Each is 500 characters, non-blank, unstructured. Whatever is decided must cover all four, or the gap simply moves. (`job_opportunity_sources.unlink_reason`, which exists today, is machine-written and out of scope.)
- **Who is affected.** A third party named in `reason` is a data subject in their own right (UA art. 8(2)(5)–(6); GDPR Art. 17 and 21). Overwriting the _actor's_ `users` row does nothing to text written about someone else. **The departing staff member is affected too**: colleagues may write "reassigned because Olena is on sick leave", which option (a) does not touch and which is health data ([GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/); UA art. 7). With two-member teams (F7), a reason written about "the other person" is about a specific identifiable colleague every time.
- **Prevention first, because it is still free (data minimisation, [Art. 5(1)(c)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/)).** Nothing writes to these columns yet and no UI exists, so the cheap options are all open and will not be cheap again once rows accumulate:
  1. **A required code from a fixed list** (for example `NOT_RELEVANT`, `DUPLICATE`, `STALE`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, `CAPACITY`), with free text **optional** and clearly secondary.
  2. **Input-side stripping**, extending the "sanitized reason" the plan already calls for (R1): remove e-mails, phone numbers and URLs on write.
  3. **A UI hint** at the field: "describe the vacancy, not a person".
  4. **Consider dropping free text entirely** where the code list suffices. If the structured reason answers the product question, the free-text column is pure liability with no offsetting benefit.
- **Keep the redaction path as a backstop. [MED]** Codes do not stop someone typing a name into whatever free text remains, and 500 characters is ample. So keep a narrow path: replace the text with a fixed token (not NULL, so the non-blank constraint and the row's meaning survive), permitted only through one privileged function, as the **only** UPDATE the trigger allows, and have each redaction **append** a `REASON_REDACTED` event recording who, when and under which request. The log then still shows _that_ something changed and by whom — provably complete rather than unchangeable, which is the defensible form of audit integrity.

## 8. Q5: Recommendation

**A hybrid. Neither (a) alone nor (b). [MED]**

1. **Keep option (a) for `actor_id` / `subject_user_id` during the retention period**, named **pseudonymisation**, not erasure. The routine must cover **everything that identifies the person**: the `users` profile and requisite fields, all `user_emails` rows, `google_id`, avatar and identity documents (S3 objects included), and **the plain-text `displayName` history in `user_audit_log.changes` (R3)**. Without R3 it does not achieve its goal even as pseudonymisation.
2. **Fix R3 in any case**, separately from this feature: add `displayName` to `SENSITIVE_FIELDS` going forward, and decide what to do with existing rows. Coder task with security-reviewer (`apps/api/src/users/**`).
3. **DECIDED — prevention at input for all four reason columns, with the narrow logged redaction path as a backstop** (§7). Owner's decision, 2026-10-17. Concretely: a required reason **code** from a fixed list, free text optional and short, **input-side stripping** of e-mails, phone numbers and URLs, and a UI hint ("describe the vacancy, not a person"). The redaction path stays as the backstop for whatever free text survives. Leave `actor_id` / `subject_user_id` NOT NULL and untouchable during retention; option (b)'s general mutability is not needed.
   **The window is open now and closes when the UI ships.** All four columns are pre-UI with no writer (R11), so codes and stripping cost a schema decision today. Once a UI exists and rows accumulate, the same change means a backfill over free text nobody can safely parse. **Decide the code list as part of the first queue-UI task, not after it.**
4. **DECIDED — end-of-retention path: DELETE the expired rows.** Owner's decision, 2026-10-17, choosing deletion over the sentinel. The reasoning **[HIGH]**: re-pointing `actor_id` to a shared "former staff" sentinel leaves `team_id` and `subject_team_id` in place, and `team_members` still records who those two people were, including after they left (R10). A reader rejoins the two tables and recovers the actor by elimination (F7, k = 2), so the sentinel destroys the pointer without destroying the linkability — the appearance of anonymisation without its substance, which is worse than doing nothing because it invites the claim that the data was anonymised.
   **The consequence to design around: aggregate counts are lost at deletion.** If any dashboard or report needs "vacancies dismissed per month" beyond two years, those figures must be **computed into a separate counters table before the rows are deleted**. Counts per period carrying no user and no team reference are genuinely anonymous and may be kept indefinitely. Decide this when the retention job is built — recovering the numbers afterwards is impossible by construction.
   The trigger needs one narrow, privileged exception used only by the retention job. **An append-only table with no expiry path is not compliant (§6.2).**
5. **Consider `ON DELETE SET NULL` instead of `RESTRICT`** for consistency with the existing audit tables (R5). Under an archive-only policy it makes no practical difference today; it removes a trap if hard deletion of users is ever introduced to meet UA art. 15(2)(2).
6. **Paperwork** — cheaper than code and it is what a regulator asks for: a written legitimate-interest balancing test; **the two-year period written into the blank ToS §6.5** (R7, text below); the **re-opening condition** from §6.1 recorded next to it; a staff privacy notice mentioning the log (UA art. 12; GDPR [Art. 13](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/)); and a template reply for partial refusal of erasure (§5.1 point 5), which under Art. 12(3) must go out within one month. The blank DPO contact in ToS §6.2 should also be filled, since a GDPR commitment with no contact point is an invitation to complain elsewhere.

   **Text for ToS §6.5.** A drafting proposal, not a final clause — the ToS is a whole-document job for a lawyer (§10), and §6.5 covers every data category, not only this log. For the queue-log row:

   > **6.5. Хранение данных.** Персональные данные хранятся не дольше, чем это необходимо для целей обработки, и не дольше сроков, установленных применимым законодательством. Для отдельных категорий установлены следующие сроки: … **записи о действиях сотрудников в очереди вакансий (кто открыл, взял, отклонил, переназначил, приостановил или выдал доступ к триажу) — 2 (два) года с момента действия, после чего записи удаляются**; … По истечении срока данные удаляются из рабочих систем; из резервных копий — в течение 30 дней (п. 6.5.1).

   Three notes on that draft: (a) it states **deletion**, not anonymisation, which matches item 4 and is the claim the system can actually honour; (b) the 30-day backup sentence pairs with item 7 — do not promise instant erasure the backups contradict; (c) the surrounding periods for other categories (documents, finance, contracts) are **not** covered by this memo and still need filling.

7. **Backups: no action, one disclosure.** Backups are pruned at **30 days** by default (R12, F8). So a deletion completes in the live database immediately and in backups within about a month, which is a defensible position under both laws. Say so in the privacy notice ("deletion takes effect immediately in the system and within 30 days in backups") rather than leaving it unstated.

**Does the owner's approved direction hold?** Yes, as an **engineering direction**. It does not hold as a claim that "pseudonymisation equals erasure", and with two-member teams that gap is wide. The direction needs items 1–4, three of which are now decided.
