## 4. Q1: Is `actor_id` personal data?

**Yes, under both laws. [HIGH]** Unchanged in r2, and F7 reinforces it (§5.1).

- **GDPR.** [Art. 4(1)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/) covers a person identifiable "by reference to an identifier", and an internal UUID that the company itself resolves through a join is exactly that. The row also says what that person did and when, which is information _relating to_ them (a record of work behaviour). Recital 26 assesses identifiability using "all the means reasonably likely to be used" ([Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/)).
- **UA.** [Law 2297-VI art. 2](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2297-17) defines personal data as information about a person "яка ідентифікована або може бути конкретно ідентифікована". The company can do this through the `users` join.
- The UUID being random does not matter. What matters is that **the company holds the key**.

## 5. Q2: Does option (a) satisfy an erasure request?

### 5.1 GDPR: option (a) is pseudonymisation, not erasure

**Conclusion: option (a) as described is not erasure. The pseudonymised log is still personal data for the company, so keeping it needs a lawful basis (§6). [HIGH — upgraded from "HIGH on the principle, MED on applying it" by F7]**

1. **The law.** Recital 26 says pseudonymised data that can be attributed to a person using additional information is still information about an identifiable person. Only anonymous information falls outside the GDPR ([Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/); definition in [Art. 4(5)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/)). EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 on pseudonymisation (adopted 16 Jan 2025 for consultation) say the same: pseudonymised data is still personal data, and pseudonymisation is a safeguard, not anonymisation. (WebSearch: https://www.hunton.com/insights/publications/edpb-advises-on-pseudonymisation-for-gdpr-compliance, collected 2026-10-17. Commentary only. I could not confirm a final adopted version, so verify against edpb.europa.eu.)
2. **Whose point of view counts.** The CJEU in _EDPS v SRB_, **C-413/23 P** (judgment of 4 Sept 2025), held that pseudonymised data may stop being personal data **for a recipient** who cannot re-identify. It stays personal data **for the controller that holds the additional information** (Commentary: https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/cjeu-clarifies-concept-of-personal-data-for-a-transfer-of-pseudonymised-data-to-third-parties and https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/cjeu-clarifies-concepts-personal-and-pseudonymised-data-implications-data-sharing, collected 2026-10-17. Law-firm summaries only; read the judgment paragraphs before relying on them). Here the company is the original controller, so the recipient-side relief does not help.
3. **Does option (a) actually destroy the "additional information"? No — and with two-member teams it is not close. [HIGH, upgraded from MED by F7]** Option (a) overwrites the key rather than keeping it separately, so the question is whether what remains is _anonymous_ under the "means reasonably likely" test. It is not. In descending order of how hard the route is to close:
   - **Structural, via team scope (new in r2).** Every scoped row carries `team_id`, and the action log also carries `subject_team_id`. A senior's team is normally **two people, the senior and one HR** (F7). So a TEAM-scoped action identifies its actor **by elimination**: if it is not the senior, it is the HR. The membership needed for that inference is in `team_members`, which is not touched by option (a) and which **keeps departed members** through the `left_at` soft delete (R10). This is not a contextual guess about colleagues' memories; it is a two-table join. **With k = 2, scope plus membership is an identifier.**
   - **R3:** `user_audit_log.changes` keeps past `displayName` values in plain text, under the same UUID. This alone defeats option (a).
   - **R2:** the same UUID is a foreign key from transactions, contracts/documents and team membership. Several of these **must** keep identity for accounting and tax reasons (§6.3), so the company can rejoin the UUID to a named person at any time.
   - **R4:** `user_emails` and `google_id` are outside the field list in the brief ("name, email, phone, photo reference and similar").

   The WP29 anonymisation tests all fail: singling out (the team-scope join), linkability (the shared UUID across tables) and inference. WP29 states plainly that "pseudonymisation is not a method of anonymisation" ([WP29 Opinion 05/2014 on Anonymisation Techniques, WP216](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2014/wp216_en.pdf)).

   **At what team size does this bite?** The argument is strongest at **k = 2**, where elimination is certain, and it stays decisive at **k ≤ 5**, where a single additional cue (a timestamp against a holiday, a role that only one member holds) usually closes the gap. From roughly **k ≥ 10** the scope stops being an identifier on its own, and the other routes above (R2, R3, R4) become the binding ones — which still defeat option (a), only less sharply. Since the answer is 2, the conclusion is firm rather than contingent. **[MED on the thresholds — these are judgment calls by analogy to k-anonymity, not a statutory test; the k = 2 conclusion itself is HIGH]**

4. **Why this does not sink the direction.** [Art. 17](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-17-gdpr/) is not a duty to delete everything on request. It applies only where one of the grounds in 17(1) is met: the data is no longer necessary (a), consent is withdrawn (b), a successful objection under Art. 21 (c), the processing is unlawful (d), or a legal obligation requires erasure (e). It also gives way to the exceptions in 17(3). If the log is still needed for a documented purpose, the controller **may refuse erasure for that period**, and option (a) is then a good minimisation step on top. Erasure becomes due when the purpose expires. At that point you need deletion or real anonymisation, and option (a) provides neither. **F5 shortens that period considerably (§6.2).**
5. **The reply to the data subject has to be honest.** Under [Art. 12(4)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-12-gdpr/), if erasure is partly refused, the person must be told why and of their right to complain. Describing option (a) to them as "your data has been erased" would be inaccurate.

### 5.2 Ukraine: the UA rule on leaving is stricter

**Conclusion: option (a) is not «знеособлення» under UA law, and the UA law presumes deletion when the relationship ends. [MED]** Unchanged in r2, and strengthened by F7: the UA definition turns on indirect identification, which is exactly what the team-scope join provides.

- Law 2297-VI art. 2 defines «знеособлення персональних даних» as removing information "які дають змогу **прямо чи опосередковано** ідентифікувати особу" (directly **or indirectly**). Because of the four routes in §5.1 point 3, indirect identification survives option (a). It is therefore not знеособлення ([Law 2297-VI](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2297-17/print), current version 14.06.2025, collected 2026-10-17).
- **Art. 15(2)(2):** personal data must be deleted or destroyed when the legal relationship between the data subject and the controller ends, «якщо інше не передбачено законом» (unless the law provides otherwise). For a departing FOP contractor, this is a **default duty to delete**, triggered by leaving and **not** by any request. The GDPR has no equivalent automatic trigger.
- **Art. 6(8):** data may be kept "у формі, що допускає ідентифікацію" (in an identifiable form) no longer than is needed for the lawful purposes.
- **Art. 8(2)(5)–(6):** the person may object to processing and may make a reasoned demand to change or destroy their data.
- **Open point. [LOW]** It is not settled whether «інше не передбачено законом» can be met by the controller's _legitimate interest_ (art. 11(1)(6)) or needs a specific statutory retention rule. I found no authoritative commentary or court practice. This decides whether the CRM's current **"archive, never delete"** policy for `users` is defensible for Ukrainian staff at all. That question is **wider than this log**, and a human lawyer should look at it (§10).
- **F4 note.** Art. 15(2)(1) also triggers deletion on expiry of a storage period "визначеного згодою … або законом". The draft ToS §6.5, where retention periods would be stated, is **blank** (R7). Filling it with the schedule in §6.2 would convert a vague position into a stated one, which helps under both laws.

### 6.3 Is there a Ukrainian retention duty that overrides erasure for this log?

- **Tax.** The Tax Code (ПКУ) п. 44.3 requires keeping documents used for tax accounting for at least 1095 days. Secondary sources report a later increase to 1825 days, which I could not confirm against the current text. (WebSearch: https://kpmg.com/ua/uk/home/media/press-releases/2023/09/novi-stroky-zberihannya-dokumentiv.html and https://profpressa.com/news/zmineno-termini-zberigannya-pervinnikh-dokumentiv-pidpisano-zakon/print, collected 2026-10-17. Verify on [zakon.rada.gov.ua ПКУ](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2755-17).) A record of who opened or took a vacancy **is not a primary accounting document** — and after F5 it is even further from one, since it feeds no payment.
- **Archive and personnel.** Minjust Order No. 578/5 of 12.04.2012 («Перелік типових документів…», registered as 571/20884) sets retention periods for standard records of legal entities. One source says it is only advisory for FOPs. (WebSearch: https://i.factor.ua/ukr/info/biudzhetnyku/stroky-zberihannia-dokumentiv/, collected 2026-10-17.) I did not find a category that clearly covers an internal CRM action log, and I did not verify individual items.
- **Labour law.** Staff are FOP contractors under civil-law contracts (R6), so employment record-keeping rules (КЗпП) generally do not apply. **[MED]**, subject to the recharacterisation risk in `.claude/knowledge/legal/domain-lessons.md`.
- **Net effect.** Keeping the log after the contract ends rests on legitimate interest alone. In Ukraine this is exactly where the art. 15(2)(2) question in §5.2 arises — and with a short retention period, the practical exposure is small.

## 7. Q4: The free-text `reason` field

**The right exists and option (a) cannot reach it [HIGH]. But the field is pre-UI, so the emphasis moves from redaction to prevention. [MED→HIGH on the remedy, upgraded because prevention is now free]**

- **Scope correction (R11).** There are **four** planned reason columns, not one: `job_queue_actions.reason`, `job_queue_dismissals.reason`, `job_queue_assignments.release_reason`, `job_team_triage_subscriptions.reason`. Each is 500 characters, non-blank, unstructured. Whatever is decided must cover all four, or the gap simply moves. (The only reason column that exists today, `job_opportunity_sources.unlink_reason`, is machine-written and not in scope.)
- **Who is affected.** A third party named in `reason` is a data subject in their own right (UA art. 8(2)(5)–(6); GDPR Art. 17 and 21 where it applies). Overwriting the _actor's_ `users` row does nothing to text written about someone else. **The departing staff member is affected too**: colleagues may write "reassigned because Olena is on sick leave", which option (a) does not touch and which is health data ([GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/); UA art. 7), raising the stakes. With two-member teams (F7), a reason written about "the other person" is about a specific identifiable colleague every time.
- **Prevention first, because it is still free (data minimisation, [Art. 5(1)(c)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-5-gdpr/)).** Nothing writes to these columns yet and no UI exists, so the cheap options are all still open, and they will not be cheap again once rows accumulate:
  1. **A required code from a fixed list** (for example `NOT_RELEVANT`, `DUPLICATE`, `STALE`, `OUT_OF_SCOPE`, `CAPACITY`), with free text **optional** and clearly secondary.
  2. **Input-side stripping**, extending the "sanitized reason" the plan already calls for (R1): remove e-mails, phone numbers and URLs on write.
  3. **A UI hint** at the field: "describe the vacancy, not a person".
  4. **Consider dropping free text entirely** where the code list suffices. If the structured reason answers the product question, the free-text column is pure liability with no offsetting benefit.
- **Keep the redaction path as a backstop, not as the main answer. [MED]** Codes do not stop someone typing a name into whatever free text remains, and 500 characters is plenty of room. So keep a narrow path: replace the text with a fixed token (not NULL, so the non-blank constraint and the row's meaning survive), permitted only through one privileged function, as the **only** UPDATE the trigger allows, and have each redaction **append** a `REASON_REDACTED` event recording who, when and under which request. The log then still shows _that_ something changed and by whom — provably complete rather than unchangeable, which is the defensible form of audit integrity.
- **Does this change the recommendation? Yes, in emphasis.** In r1 the redaction path was item 3 of the recommendation. It is now item 3 **behind** prevention, and if the owner drops free text entirely it becomes unnecessary for three of the four columns. The hybrid's shape is unchanged.

## 8. Q5: Recommendation

**A hybrid. Neither (a) alone nor (b). [MED]**

1. **Keep option (a) for `actor_id` / `subject_user_id` during the retention period**, named **pseudonymisation**, not erasure. The routine must cover **everything that identifies the person**: the `users` profile and requisite fields, all `user_emails` rows, `google_id`, avatar and identity documents (S3 objects included), and **the plain-text `displayName` history in `user_audit_log.changes` (R3)**. Without R3 it does not achieve its goal even as pseudonymisation.
2. **Fix R3 in any case**, separately from this feature: add `displayName` to `SENSITIVE_FIELDS` going forward, and decide what to do with existing rows. Coder task with security-reviewer (`apps/api/src/users/**`).
3. **Prevention at input for all four reason columns, with a narrow logged redaction path as a backstop** (§7). Leave `actor_id` / `subject_user_id` NOT NULL and untouchable during retention. Option (b)'s general mutability is not needed.
4. **End-of-retention path: DELETE the expired rows. The sentinel alternative does not work here. [changed in r2, MED→HIGH]** In r1 I offered two options — delete, or re-point `actor_id` to a shared "former staff" sentinel. **F7 rules out the second.** Re-pointing leaves `team_id` and `subject_team_id` in place, and `team_members` still records who those two people were, including after they left (R10). The reader rejoins the two tables and recovers the actor by elimination, so the sentinel destroys the pointer without destroying the linkability — the appearance of anonymisation without its substance, which is worse than doing nothing because it invites the claim that the data was anonymised. The alternatives are:
   - **DELETE the expired rows** — destroys linkability, simple to verify, and loses only aggregate counts. **Recommended.**
   - Keep rows but null **both** the actor columns **and** the team scope — leaves a row that no longer says who or where, which is close to worthless. If the aggregate matters, compute it into a separate counters table before deleting; counts per period with no user or team reference are genuinely anonymous.
     Either way the trigger needs a second narrow, privileged exception used only by the retention job. **An append-only table with no expiry path is not compliant (§6.2).**
5. **Consider `ON DELETE SET NULL` instead of `RESTRICT`** for consistency with the existing audit tables (R5). Under an archive-only policy it makes no practical difference today; it removes a trap if hard deletion of users is ever introduced to meet UA art. 15(2)(2).
6. **Paperwork** — cheaper than code and it is what a regulator asks for: a written legitimate-interest balancing test; the retention schedule from §6.2 **written into the blank ToS §6.5** (R7); the **re-opening condition** from §6.1 recorded next to it; a staff privacy notice mentioning the log (UA art. 12; GDPR [Art. 13](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-13-gdpr/)); and a template reply for partial refusal of erasure (§5.1 point 5). The blank DPO contact in ToS §6.2 should also be filled, since a GDPR commitment with no contact point is an invitation to complain elsewhere.
7. **Backups: no action, one disclosure. [new in r2]** F8 confirms backups are pruned at **30 days** by default (R12). So a deletion completes in the live database immediately and in backups within about a month, which is a defensible position under both laws. Say so in the privacy notice ("deletion takes effect immediately in the system and within 30 days in backups") rather than leaving it unstated. No design change — this was a worry in r1 and the answer closed it.

**Does the owner's condition hold?** Yes, as an **engineering direction**, and r2 makes it hold more firmly on a shorter retention period. It does not hold as a claim that "pseudonymisation equals erasure", and with two-member teams that gap is wider than r1 suggested. The direction needs items 1–4.
