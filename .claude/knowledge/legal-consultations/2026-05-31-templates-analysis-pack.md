### Section A — SENIOR contract: FULL EXPERT ANALYSIS

#### A.1 Strengths

The existing draft has several well-written blocks that are worth keeping in a reform:

1. **A clear subject-matter definition (clause 1.1).** It directly names "Services for preparation for future employment" — this is key for the characterization as a **service contract (a services agreement)** under Art. 901 of the Civil Code of Ukraine, and not as a placement agency (where there is an additional regulatory layer). The list 1.1.1–1.1.5 is quite concrete and operationally justified.
2. **The recursive service trigger upon loss of the IT contract (clause 5.2).** A reasonable business logic: if the SENIOR loses the contract — the Provider again provides search services, during this period does not receive payment. This balances the risk distribution and softens the argument about an "unjustified advantage" of the Provider.
3. **A detailed acceptance procedure (clause 4.2).** Tacit acceptance after 5 days — a standard UA pattern, OK for a service contract. This is based on Art. 530 of the Civil Code of Ukraine (a reasonable term of performance).
4. **Confidentiality 5 years (section 7).** Reasonable — the lower edge of typical IT NDA terms. In particular the list (clause 7.3) includes "ways of accessing systems, intellectual property objects" — this captures business-critical assets.
5. **Electronic document flow via messengers (clause 5.3, clause 12.3).** Corresponds to [Art. 11 of Law No. 675-VIII](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/675-19) "On Electronic Commerce" on the conclusion of an electronic contract. Practically OK, but the lawyer should check the exact wording.
6. **An IP transfer clause (clause 8.1).** There is a base pattern, although it needs refinement (see A.3.6).
7. **Force majeure includes war (clause 6.2).** Critical in UA 2026 — without it, upon mobilization, SENIOR/JUNIOR contract terminations may become disputed.

#### A.2 Red flags + critical risks

This is the most important part of the analysis. The list is prioritized by severity.

##### A.2.1 [P0 — CRITICAL] Recharacterization risk (an employment relationship)

**The nature of the risk:** The structure of the SENIOR contract with a 74-84% commission on the SENIOR's income, in which the Provider (Maksym-FOP) has the right:

- to request from the SENIOR documentation about the income (clauses 2.1.6, 3.2.5, 3.2.6)
- to coordinate the team (HR Salamakha hires under Maksym)
- to have a 24-month term with automatic extension (clause 11.1, 5.2)
- to control quality (clause 5.1.1 — the 84% rate upon complaints)

creates a **significant probability of recharacterization** as a concealed employment relationship between **Maksym and the SENIOR** in a DPS audit.

According to [Art. 21 of the Labor Code of Ukraine](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/322-08), an employment contract is an agreement where "the employee undertakes to perform work subject to the internal labor schedule, and the employer — to pay wages and provide working conditions".

Although the formal parties are FOP-FOP, **substance over form** is the main principle of the DPS in control. According to [the draft law of the Ministry of Social Policy 2026](https://biz.nv.ua/ukr/markets/7-oznak-trudovih-vidnosin-kompaniji-ta-fop-za-yaki-shtrafuvatimut-minsocpolitiki-zaproponuvalo-zakonoproekt-50006656.html) (WebSearch: collection date 2026-05-31), 7 signs of an employment relationship are defined. If an FOP-FOP contract falls under at least 3 of the 7 — this is a trigger for a recalculation.

**Specifically in our SENIOR draft a minimum of 3 signs are breached:**

| #   | Sign of an employment relationship (Ministry of Social Policy 2026) | How it manifests in the SENIOR draft                                                                                 |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Periodic payments (2+) for work                                     | Clause 5.1 — a monthly 74% of each incoming payment of the SENIOR                                                    |
| 2   | Personal performance under management                               | Clause 1.1.4 — the Provider "carries out the full process of organizing the passing of interviews", i.e. coordinates |
| 3   | The payment = the sole/main source of income (75%+ over 6 months)   | Clause 5.1.1 (84%) — practically all the SENIOR's income is controlled by the contract                               |
| 5   | Work on projects determined by the other party                      | Clause 1.1.1–1.1.5 — the Provider determines what to prepare the SENIOR for                                          |

**Financial impact of recharacterization (a theoretical worst case in a DPS audit):**

Upon a recalculation over a 3-year period (max), per 1 SENIOR with an annual income of 600k UAH (typical):

- PIT: 18% × 1.8M = **324k UAH** + a 25% penalty = **+81k UAH**
- Military levy: 1.5% × 1.8M = **27k UAH** + a 25% penalty = **+6.75k UAH**
- USC: 22% × 1.8M = **396k UAH** + a 25% penalty = **+99k UAH**
- A penalty for an unregistered employee Art. 265 of the Labor Code of Ukraine: 10 minimum wages × 8647 UAH = **86,470 UAH per each** per [Factor Academy 2026 update](https://factor.academy/blog/trudovi-shtrafi-na-2026-rik/) (WebSearch: 2026-05-31)
- An administrative fine Art. 41 of the Code of Administrative Offenses

**Total per 1 SENIOR over 3 years: ~1.02M UAH.** With 10 SENIORs = **₴10M direct exposure** + a criminal risk per Art. 212 of the Tax Code of Ukraine at a sum > 4.99M (memory lesson [P0]).

⚠️ **This is Hard Refuse Zone § 4** (escalation-zones.md) — sums > 100k UAH require a tax consultant. The figures above are an order-of-magnitude estimate, not final.

##### A.2.2 [P0 — CRITICAL] GDPR + UA 2297-VI breach in clause 2.1.6 + clause 3.2.6

**The nature of the risk:** Clause 2.1.6 grants the Provider the right **to make requests to the DPS "regarding the income received by the Customer"**. Clause 3.2.6 obliges the SENIOR to provide **statements from bank accounts (including currency ones), extracts from Wise/Payoneer/Revolut, screenshots of banking applications**.

This is not just a compliance verification — it is a **disclosure to a third party of special categories of personal data** (financial information).

According to [Law No. 2297-VI "On the Protection of Personal Data"](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2297-17):

- **Art. 11** — the processing of personal data is carried out on the basis of the consent of the subject OR in other cases determined by law
- **The consent must be:** freely given, specific (with a defined purpose), informed, unambiguous (WebSearch: collection date 2026-05-31)
- **A change in the purpose of processing** requires new consent

**Problems in the current wording:**

1. **Clause 2.1.6** ("By their signature in this Agreement the Customer grants the Provider the full right to carry out the requests specified in this clause") — a claim of blanket consent. **This does NOT meet the requirements of "specific" consent** — it is not defined: to exactly which DPS bodies, with what frequency, which specific data, for what term, what procedure for notifying the SENIOR.
2. **A DPS request from a third party about the income of an individual** — this is a **request for confidential information**. The DPS is not obliged to satisfy it. According to [Art. 17 of the Tax Code of Ukraine](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/2755-17), tax secrets are disclosed only in clearly defined cases. Maksym as an outside third party is not one of them. That is, **clause 2.1.6 is in practice unenforceable by the DPS**, but creates a misleading signal to the SENIOR that they are obliged to accept this.
3. **The clause 3.2.6 disclosure** — bank statements = **special category personal data**. Transfer without adequate safeguards (encryption, retention period, security measures, a breach notification protocol) = a direct breach of 2297-VI.
4. **If the SENIOR serves clients from the EU** — additionally GDPR concerns. Maksym as a processor of unrelated data of the SENIOR's clients through bank statements = a controller under [GDPR Art.4](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/), requires a DPA + a lawful basis (probably legitimate interest with a balancing test, but vulnerable).

**Sanctions for a breach of Law No. 2297-VI:**

- An administrative fine Art. 188-39 of the Code of Administrative Offenses: 1000-2000 non-taxable minimums (₴17-34k for a repeat violation)
- Criminal liability Art. 182 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine upon illegal collection/disclosure (rare in practice, but the exposure exists)

##### A.2.3 [P1 — HIGH] Unconscionability 84% post-complaints (clause 5.1.1) + post-termination (clause 10.1.1)

**The nature of the risk:** Clause 5.1.1 — the commission level jumps to 84% upon 4+ complaints about juniors. Clause 10.1.1 — 84% continues to be paid even after the termination of the agreement, "until the full cessation of cooperation with the IT company".

This may be recognized as an **unfair condition** under the theory of an "unjustified advantage" (Art. 627-628 of the Civil Code of Ukraine — the principle of freedom of contract vs fairness). Although a direct precedent on an 84% rate is hard to find, there is an analogy via [the Supreme Court Rulings on Art. 1212 of the Civil Code of Ukraine](https://supreme.court.gov.ua/userfiles/media/new_folder_for_uploads/supreme/ogliady/Daidgest_maino_st1212.pdf) (WebSearch: collection date 2026-05-31).

**Specific concerns:**

1. **84% upon complaints — punitive not compensatory.** This does not reflect the actual cost of Maksym's increased service effort, but a punitive shift in the economics of the deal. May be argued as an **abuse of right** (Art. 13 of the Civil Code of Ukraine).
2. **Clause 10.1.1 post-termination 84% — substantially problematic.** If the agreement is terminated, but the SENIOR continues to receive income from a contract found through Maksym — the 84% protection is limited by the logic "Maksym earned it, a placement service".
   - However — **the duration is not limited**. "Until the full cessation of cooperation with the IT company" can last for years.
   - A Ukrainian court may apply **Art. 1212 of the Civil Code of Ukraine "unjust enrichment"** as a reverse argument — Maksym receives a remuneration without providing a service (since the agreement is terminated).
3. **Comparison reference:** typical IT recruitment placement fees — 15-25% of the annual salary, a ONE-TIME payment, not an ongoing 84%. **74% ongoing — an outlier in the market** and this reinforces the recharacterization risk (resembles an employer-employee revenue split, not a placement service).

##### A.2.4 [P1 — HIGH] Missing non-circumvention as a substantive clause

**The nature of the risk:** The confidentiality section 7 covers the non-disclosure of information. But there is **no non-circumvention** — a prohibition for the SENIOR to directly bypass Maksym and conclude contracts with companies that Maksym found, but without a commission.

**Business impact:** The SENIOR after 3-6 months can simply:

1. Terminate the agreement (10 days notice, clause 10.1)
2. Sign a **new** contract with the same IT company after 3-6 months
3. Argue that "this is a new contract, not the one Maksym found" — Maksym has no mechanism to contest this

Clause 10.1.1 tries to cover this scenario (84% continues) but:

- It works only if the SENIOR continues to receive income from **that specific contract**
- If the SENIOR signed a **new** contract with the same company — clause 10.1.1 does not trigger

**Enforceability of non-compete clauses in Ukraine (per [go-advocate.com 2026 analysis](https://go-advocate.com/zakonnist-non-compete-agreement-v-ukrajini/), WebSearch: collection date 2026-05-31):**

- The constitutional guarantee of the right to work (Art. 43 of the Constitution) makes a broad non-compete unenforceable
- Lexology UA (via 1 of the search results) — Ukrainian courts treat an NCA as "moral incentives rather than enforceable legal instruments"
- For validity are needed: **a time limit ≤12 months, a geographic scope, an exhaustive list of activities, material compensation**

**Recommended solution:** the lawyer should draft a narrowly-scoped non-circumvention clause:

- Specific named companies (a list, updatable)
- 12-24 months post-termination
- With **compensation** for the SENIOR (for example, 25% of the base rate for the non-circumvention period) — this makes the clause enforceable
- A liquidated damages formula (for example, 50% of the income the SENIOR earned circumventing × 2 years)

##### A.2.5 [P1 — HIGH] Missing failed-placement scenario

**The nature of the risk:** The agreement assumes the placement will be successful. The following are not covered:

- What if the SENIOR did not get offers in 3, 6, 12 months

**Practical impact:** The SENIOR can argue that Maksym "did not provide the service" and refuse to sign invoices even if HR Salamakha spent hours on screening.

##### B.2.1 [P1 — HIGH] Recharacterization risk — $800 monthly base

**The nature of the risk:** $800/month fixed base — this is a **classic employee compensation pattern**. According to [Art. 21 of the Labor Code of Ukraine](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/322-08) + the 7 signs of the Ministry of Social Policy (as described in A.2.1), a fixed monthly retainer breaches one of the critical signs — "**periodic payments**".

If HR Salamakha:

- Works 8 hours daily
- Performs tasks determined by Maksym

2. **Pivot to per-hire-only compensation** — remove the $800 base, keep $350/hire + a retainer for "consultation" 4 hours/month fixed

##### B.2.2 [P1 — HIGH] 3-year confidentiality enforceability — depends on compensation

Per [go-advocate.com analysis 2026](https://go-advocate.com/zakonnist-non-compete-agreement-v-ukrajini/) (WebSearch: collection date 2026-05-31), for the enforceability of restrictive covenants in UA:

- ≤ 12 months optimal
- **Material compensation required** for enforceability
- Geographic + activity scope clearly defined

**3-year confidentiality without extra compensation** may be challenged as excessive. Counter-argument: an NDA (non-disclosure) is not a non-compete, so restrictive covenant rules may not apply. But:

- If the confidentiality clause includes "non-solicitation" of candidates (HR Salamakha cannot hire the same candidates for another client) — this is a **de facto non-compete**, therefore it **must have compensation**.
- If it is a pure NDA (information cannot be disclosed) — 3 years OK, but enforcement via Art. 231-232 of the Criminal Code of Ukraine requires **proving that the information was a trade secret** ([Art. 36 of the Commercial Code of Ukraine](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/436-15) definition).

**Recommendation:** The lawyer should clearly distinguish:

- A pure NDA (3 years OK)
- Non-solicitation (1 year maximum with compensation)
- Non-compete (12 months with compensation if needed)

##### B.2.3 [P2 — MEDIUM] IP "upon creation" — automatic assignment uncertainty

UA law does not recognize automatic IP assignment "upon creation" as a universal principle. [Art. 1107 of the Civil Code of Ukraine](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/435-15) — the distribution of IP rights requires:

- A separate license agreement OR
- An agreement on the transfer of exclusive rights (a deed of assignment) — notarization is not mandatory, but a written detailed description is mandatory
- The transfer may be in the main agreement, but requires a description of the specific objects

**Risk:** If it just says "IP to the Client upon creation" without a detailed description of the objects — Salamakha may argue that specific materials (for example, the candidate scoring rubric, the interview transcripts methodology) are not covered.

**Recommendation:** The lawyer should:

1. List the specific categories of IP (candidate DBs, interview scripts, scoring rubrics, sourcing strategies)
2. Add an "obligation to execute supplementary deeds at the Client's request" with liquidated damages for refusal
3. Pre-execute a generic IP assignment for **future works** with a backstop clause

The summary indicates "bonuses cease upon notice (existing projects continue)" — this is a reasonable carve-out.

**Recommendation:** Distinguish:

##### B.2.5 [P2 — MEDIUM] 1-year term — does it auto-renew?

The summary does not indicate a renewal mechanism. If the contract expires 16.03.2027 without auto-renewal — Maksym needs to be reminded/sign manually. Risks:

- A delay in renewal → a gap → potential re-characterization (continued work without a contract)
- The possibility to renegotiate in both directions (HR may request more $$, Maksym may decrease)

#### C.5 Common pitfalls in analogous templates

#### E.5 Common pitfalls in analogous templates

1. **Copy-paste US TOS** — does not apply the UA framework, gaps in consumer protection, GDPR compliance
2. **Vague IP ownership** — disputes inevitable about user-generated content
3. **No version history** — the user does not know what changed, the ToS unenforceable
4. **No cookie consent** — a UA + EU compliance breach automatic
5. **No data retention periods** — a GDPR breach
6. **No transfers outside the EU clauses** — if AWS US-based hosting → instant non-compliance
7. **Excessive limitation of liability** — UA consumer protection invalidates it
8. **No DPO contact** — GDPR Art.37 if required (for public authorities or large-scale monitoring — for our scale maybe not, but best practice)
9. **No DPIA mention** — GDPR Art.35 required for high-risk processing (USDT wallets — likely qualifies)
